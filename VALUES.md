# The LoLLMS Constitution

**The principles that must never be traded away**

**Status:** Foundational project charter
**Applies to:** LoLLMS, its official distributions, services, hosted offerings, subsidiaries, maintainers, officers, and any organization exercising control over the project
**Purpose:** Preserve the philosophical, ethical, and social purpose of LoLLMS through growth, commercialization, investment, acquisition, and changes in leadership.

> Technology must serve human beings, not turn them into products.
>
> LoLLMS exists to increase human autonomy. It must never become an instrument for taking that autonomy away.

This document is not a marketing statement. It is a statement of identity and a set of constraints on the decisions that may be made in the name of LoLLMS.

If a profitable opportunity conflicts with these values, the opportunity must be rejected, redesigned, or pursued outside the LoLLMS project without compromising its users.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 1. Why LoLLMS exists

LoLLMS was not created merely to sell software, capture a market, or win a competition between AI platforms.

It was created from a deeper conviction: individuals, families, communities, researchers, and organizations should be able to own and control the digital environments on which their lives and work increasingly depend.

People should not have to surrender their conversations, memories, relationships, creations, knowledge, or autonomy to a handful of corporations in exchange for convenient tools.

LoLLMS exists to make another path practical.

It aims to provide a complete, adaptable, multimodal and collaborative AI environment that people can operate, understand, modify, host, and govern themselves.

The project must remain useful to an individual or a family, not only to a company with an IT department and an enterprise budget.

A family using LoLLMS to preserve and share photographs, memories, conversations, and everyday moments is not a secondary or accidental use case. It expresses the purpose of the project: technology that belongs to the people who use it.

**Our central commitment**

People must remain the owners and decision-makers of their digital lives.

LoLLMS is a tool for that sovereignty. It must never become a new gatekeeper.

## 2. The non-negotiable principles

The following principles are the protected core of LoLLMS. They apply regardless of the project's size, ownership structure, financial performance, or commercial success.

### 2.1 User sovereignty

Users must retain meaningful control over their digital environment.

They must be able to choose where LoLLMS runs, which models and services it uses, what data it stores, who can access that data, and when information is shared.

The project must favor architectures that make this control technically real, rather than relying only on promises or contractual language.

A user must not be forced into a particular cloud provider, model provider, identity provider, or commercial ecosystem to retain access to their own information.

### 2.2 Privacy by architecture

Privacy must be a design property, not a premium feature.

- Local and self-hosted operation must remain first-class deployment options.
- Private data must not be collected for advertising, behavioral profiling, or unrelated commercial exploitation.
- No hidden telemetry, undisclosed data export, or covert surveillance may be introduced.
- Any optional telemetry must be documented, minimized, and subject to informed user control.
- External services must be clearly identified when they receive data.
- Sensitive information must not be transmitted to an external model or service without an explicit and understandable user decision.
- Administrators must have clear controls over retention, deletion, backups, and access.

LoLLMS must not claim that data never leaves a device when a user has configured external services or remote infrastructure. It must explain the actual data flows honestly.

### 2.3 No monetization of personal intimacy

Users' private conversations, family photographs, personal memories, social relationships, documents, and behavioral data are not a commercial asset to be harvested.

LoLLMS must never:

- Sell personal data.
- Sell access to private conversations or memories.
- Use private content for advertising.
- Build advertising profiles from user activity.
- Make personal data a condition of financing the project.
- Introduce business models that depend on exploiting the attention or intimacy of users.

A user's trust is not inventory.

### 2.4 Freedom to use, inspect, modify, and leave

LoLLMS must remain an open-source project under a license that preserves the freedoms to use, study, modify, and redistribute the software.

- The project must not use proprietary extensions, license tricks, or technical restrictions to make the open-source core deliberately incomplete or unusable.
- Users must have practical ways to export their data and leave the ecosystem without losing access to their own work.
- Open standards, documented interfaces, interoperability, and replaceable components should be preferred over artificial lock-in.
- The exact open-source license and any future licensing changes must be reviewed for compatibility with this principle. No change may be used to revoke freedoms already granted to existing recipients.

### 2.5 No artificial scarcity in the essential core

The essential capabilities that make LoLLMS a sovereign personal and organizational AI environment must remain available without a per-user or per-seat license fee.

The project may offer paid services, hosting, support, integration, training, maintenance, or specialized development. Those services must not be used to hold users' data, identity, or essential freedoms hostage.

A paid service may provide convenience, professional guarantees, or additional resources. It must not become the only practical way to exercise the fundamental freedoms promised by LoLLMS.

### 2.6 Human agency before automation

AI must augment human capability, not quietly replace human judgment or make people dependent on opaque systems.

- Users must be able to understand when they are interacting with an AI system, inspect relevant sources, challenge conclusions, change models, and disable automation.
- Agents must operate within explicit permissions. Consequential actions must have appropriate human oversight, reversibility, and auditability.
- LoLLMS must not manipulate users into accepting an AI's output as unquestionable truth.

### 2.7 Intellectual honesty and epistemic pluralism

No AI model, provider, company, institution, or individual has a monopoly on truth.

LoLLMS must support the comparison of models, sources, and perspectives. Its information tools should make it possible to inspect evidence, identify uncertainty, distinguish fact from interpretation, and recognize conflicts of interest.

- The project must not market its agents as perfectly unbiased. No model or information source is perfectly unbiased.
- The goal is not to replace one authority with another. The goal is to give users the means to investigate, compare, reason, and decide.
- Where an agent gathers information from the Web, source attribution, traceability, and clear distinctions between retrieved information and generated analysis should be preferred.

### 2.8 No attention extraction

LoLLMS must not reproduce the worst incentives of commercial social networks.

- Its social features must serve communication, meaningful sharing, discovery, learning, and relationships.
- They must not be optimized to maximize compulsive use, outrage, anxiety, or endless engagement for advertising revenue.
- Users should retain meaningful control over their feeds, recommendations, notifications, visibility, and social boundaries.
- A feed should help people find what matters to them. It should not decide what they should care about.

### 2.9 Accessibility and technological inclusion

LoLLMS must strive to remain usable by individuals and organizations with different budgets, levels of expertise, hardware, and infrastructure.

- It should support local hardware and a range of deployment environments rather than assuming that every user can afford a large cloud subscription or high-end accelerator.
- Documentation, reasonable defaults, modularity, and community contributions are part of accessibility.
- The project must not confuse technological sophistication with human progress.

### 2.10 Cooperation over extraction

LoLLMS should strengthen the open-source ecosystem and cooperate with other projects where doing so benefits users.

- It must not use its position to create unnecessary dependencies, exploit contributors, appropriate community work without respect for its license, or undermine other open projects through deception.
- Competition is legitimate. Artificial scarcity, deceptive claims, and ecosystem capture are not.

## 3. The family and community are first-class users

LoLLMS must never be designed exclusively around enterprise procurement.

- A family should be able to operate its own digital environment and decide what it shares with whom.
- A community should be able to collaborate without automatically exposing its members to advertising, profiling, or unrelated commercial surveillance.
- A small association, independent researcher, school, laboratory, or local organization should be able to deploy useful capabilities without first negotiating an enterprise contract.

The following are legitimate and central uses of LoLLMS:

- Sharing family photographs, memories, and everyday moments.
- Maintaining private conversations and group spaces.
- Building a personal or family knowledge environment.
- Using personal agents to research, organize, and explain information.
- Creating and editing text, images, audio, video, and other media.
- Collaborating with trusted people and communities.
- Running AI locally or on infrastructure controlled by the user.

The project must protect these uses as it grows.

## 4. UBI 2.0: technology must distribute prosperity, not concentrate it

LoLLMS is rooted in a broader vision of technological and economic transformation.

Artificial intelligence and automation have the potential to produce extraordinary abundance. But greater productive capacity does not automatically create greater human freedom. Without deliberate choices, the gains can concentrate in the hands of those who own the infrastructure, data, and capital.

The ideas described as Universal Basic Income 2.0 (UBI 2.0) on lollms.com express a more ambitious response than a traditional minimum-income safety net.

UBI 2.0 is a social and economic vision, not a feature of the LoLLMS application and not a claim that a complete public policy has already been proven. It is a direction for thinking about how the benefits of automation can be shared.

### 4.1 Universal participation in technological prosperity

People should have a meaningful stake in the wealth generated by technologies built on collective knowledge, infrastructure, and human contribution.

The productivity gains of AI should not flow exclusively to a small group of infrastructure owners.

### 4.2 Universal capital endowments and shared ownership

The UBI 2.0 vision explores universal capital endowments: giving every person a stake in productive assets, including AI infrastructure, so that technological progress can generate broadly shared benefits.

Models such as public or community-owned AI trusts, cooperative ownership, and long-term universal funds deserve serious exploration.

### 4.3 Recognition of human contributions that markets undervalue

Caregiving, mentoring, education, community service, creativity, ecological stewardship, and the transmission of knowledge are essential to society.

Their value cannot be reduced to the number of tasks automated or the amount of revenue generated.

UBI 2.0 explores ways to recognize and support these contributions, including universal purpose dividends and exploration stipends.

### 4.4 Data rights and data dividends

People should have meaningful rights regarding the data they generate and contribute.

The UBI 2.0 vision raises the possibility that the economic value derived from personal and collective data should be shared with the people whose contributions made it possible.

This must never be interpreted as permission for LoLLMS to sell its users' private data. On the contrary, user sovereignty and informed control are prerequisites for any fair data economy.

### 4.5 Human development beyond economic survival

If automation reduces the amount of labor required to meet material needs, society should not define human worth solely through employment.

People should have greater opportunities to learn, create, care, explore, participate in their communities, and pursue meaningful projects.

Technology should give people more time and agency, not turn them into passive consumers.

### 4.6 Avoiding the dependency trap

Material security alone is not enough.

A society in which people lose their skills, curiosity, critical thinking, relationships, and capacity to act would not be a successful technological civilization.

LoLLMS should therefore support learning, experimentation, creativity, human interaction, and independent thought. AI should be a cognitive instrument, not a substitute for a person's entire intellectual life.

### 4.7 The connection to LoLLMS

LoLLMS cannot implement UBI 2.0 by itself. Its role is more concrete and more modest: to help democratize access to AI capabilities, lower barriers to participation, enable local and cooperative infrastructure, and give individuals and communities more control over the tools that increasingly shape economic life.

The benefits of automation should expand human freedom. They should not create a new digital aristocracy.

## 5. Commercial sustainability is allowed. Selling the mission is not.

LoLLMS may become a company, receive investment, employ people, pay salaries, enter commercial partnerships, and generate revenue.

There is no contradiction between financial sustainability and the project's values.

The contradiction appears when financial returns are allowed to override the purpose for which the project exists.

**The following commercial activities are compatible with this charter:**

- Paid installation, migration, and deployment services.
- Professional support and maintenance contracts.
- Training, consulting, and certification.
- Managed hosting for users who explicitly choose it.
- Custom integrations and development.
- Hardware and infrastructure partnerships that preserve user choice.
- Optional enterprise administration and operational services.
- Grants, donations, sponsorships, and research partnerships with transparent conditions.

**The following are incompatible with this charter:**

- Selling or licensing access to users' private data.
- Introducing advertising based on personal activity.
- Making essential sovereignty features available only through recurring seat licenses.
- Deliberately degrading the free core to force commercial upgrades.
- Creating technical lock-in to prevent users from leaving.
- Secretly favoring a model provider, cloud provider, investor, or commercial partner.
- Giving investors or partners access to private user content.
- Making the project dependent on surveillance or attention extraction.
- Removing the freedoms already granted by the open-source license.

### Investors are partners, not owners of the mission

An investor may finance development and receive a fair financial return under agreed terms.

An investor must not acquire the right to redefine the fundamental purpose of LoLLMS.

No investment agreement should grant an investor unilateral authority to:

- Change the protected principles in this document.
- Close the essential open-source core.
- Introduce personal-data monetization.
- Force users into a proprietary cloud.
- Remove local or self-hosted operation as a first-class option.
- Convert essential features into compulsory paid subscriptions.
- Override the project's privacy and transparency commitments.

If an investor requires any of these conditions, that investment is incompatible with LoLLMS.

The project must be willing to refuse capital rather than accept capital that requires it to betray its purpose.

## 6. Governance: values need structural protection

A declaration is meaningful only if the organization has mechanisms to uphold it.

If LoLLMS becomes a company, these principles should be reflected in binding governance documents and technical practices.

The following safeguards are recommended:

### 6.1 A protected charter

This document should be adopted as a formal project charter.

Any amendment to its protected principles should require a process more demanding than an ordinary management or shareholder decision.

### 6.2 Independent stewardship

The project should explore a foundation, nonprofit entity, trust, or another independent stewardship structure capable of protecting the open-source mission.

The legal structure should be designed so that no single investor, shareholder, executive, or acquirer can unilaterally remove the protected principles.

### 6.3 Reserved matters

The company's articles, shareholder agreements, and investment documents should identify changes to the protected core as reserved matters.

Such changes should require approval from an independent mission-protecting body and meaningful representation of the open-source community.

### 6.4 Open-source continuity

- The core repository should remain available under its established open-source license.
- The organization should maintain reproducible releases, public source history, documented build and deployment processes, and reasonable continuity plans.
- Where feasible, critical infrastructure and project assets should not depend on a single commercial account or individual.

### 6.5 Transparency

- Material changes affecting privacy, telemetry, licensing, data flows, hosting, or user autonomy should be publicly documented.
- Security incidents affecting users should be handled responsibly and communicated with appropriate transparency.
- Commercial relationships that materially influence product direction should be disclosed.

### 6.6 Community participation

Users and contributors should have clear channels to raise concerns, propose changes, challenge decisions, and participate in the evolution of the project.

Community participation must be meaningful, not merely symbolic.

### 6.7 Periodic values review

The project should periodically publish a review of its compliance with this charter.

The review should address, at minimum:

- Whether the essential core remains open source.
- Whether local and self-hosted use remains practical.
- Whether any new data collection has been introduced.
- Whether users retain meaningful data portability.
- Whether commercial offerings create artificial restrictions.
- Whether governance safeguards remain effective.
- Whether the project is still serving individuals and communities, not only enterprise customers.

**Important:** This charter is a project-level ethical commitment. It does not by itself create legally enforceable rights or override corporate law, shareholder agreements, or intellectual-property ownership.
